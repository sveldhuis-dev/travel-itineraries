Your trip, hour by hour

How each day of the land tour splits across sightseeing, shopping, travel, meals, downtime and sleep. Oct 25 to Nov 8, with the flights out and home, built from the Sept 16 final itinerary and your Delta booking.

Where the days are full

Oct 28 in Delhi (5.5 h of sights, from Humayun's Tomb to Akshardham) and Oct 31 in Jaipur (6.2 h, Amber Fort through the evening aarti) are the heaviest sightseeing days. Oct 29 is the longest travel day at 8 h, with both train legs to Agra and back.

Where it breathes

Nov 4 in Udaipur has about 7 h of open time, including a free afternoon and evening. Nov 7 in Mumbai also has about 7 h, and the first Delhi afternoon is open if you take the rest group.

Sleep is shorter than it looks

Oct 27 starts with a 2:40 AM landing, so that day has only about 5 h of sleep. Oct 29 starts with a 6:30 AM breakfast, and Nov 7 ends with a late airport transfer for the 2:25 AM flight home.

What to Bring

Packing List

Late Oct–early Nov in North India (Delhi/Agra/Jaipur): warm days (75–85°F), cool mornings/evenings (50–60°F), low humidity. Udaipur similar. Mumbai is warmer and more humid year-round (80–90°F). Modest dress required for temples.

Clothing

  • Lightweight layers — light jacket/sweater for cool mornings (Delhi/Jaipur/Udaipur)
  • Modest clothing for temples — shoulders/knees covered
  • Comfortable cotton/linen shirts for Mumbai heat
  • Nicer outfits for the two prepaid special dinners (Jaipur, Udaipur) and heritage-restaurant dinners
  • Scarf or shawl (temple visits, AC, dust)

Footwear

  • Comfortable walking shoes — forts and Old Delhi rickshaw route have real walking
  • Slip-on shoes — many temples require shoe removal
  • Sandals for hotel/evening wear

Health & Sun

  • Sunscreen SPF 50+
  • Sunglasses, sun hat
  • Hand sanitizer, wet wipes
  • Prescription medications + copies of prescriptions
  • Consult travel doctor — possible vaccinations/malaria prophylaxis
  • Stomach medication (Imodium, Pepto) as precaution

Photography

  • Camera + extra batteries
  • Wide angle lens — Taj Mahal, forts, palaces
  • Extra memory cards
  • Portable charger

Practical

  • Universal power adapter (India: Type C/D/M, 230V)
  • Cash — small USD bills + plan to exchange for INR
  • Copies of passport, eVisa, and E-Arrival Card printed
  • Travel insurance documents (Travelex)
  • Earplugs (city noise, early flights/trains)
  • Phone with WhatsApp installed — group coordination chat

Documents

  • Passports — valid 6+ months from travel date
  • eVisa confirmation + E-Arrival Card (submit within 72 hrs of landing)
  • Flight confirmation GV5C9D
  • JourneyQuest itinerary + emergency contacts (Sanjeev Sharma, Himani Rohilla)
  • Travelex insurance policy details
  • Hotel confirmations (Oberoi ×3, Taj Mahal Palace, JW Marriott Mumbai Sahar)

Temple EtiquetteShoes off before entering most temples. Shoulders and knees covered. A light scarf is useful for impromptu head coverings.
🔐
Akshardham SecurityBags and electronics aren't allowed inside — budget the full hour for entry, security, and the visit.
💧
Water & Food SafetyPremium bottled water is included throughout. Stick to bottled/filtered water even for teeth-brushing. Hotel/curated restaurants are very safe; street food is tempting but higher risk.
💰
Tipping CultureIndia has an active tipping culture for guides, drivers, and hotel staff. Budget roughly $20–30/day per couple for guide and driver combined, plus small tips for hotel staff.
🛍️
Shopping NegotiationBazaar shopping (Chandni Chowk, Jaipur, Udaipur markets) involves negotiation as standard practice. Curated stops like Sabyasachi and Kashmir Loom are fixed-price.
📅
Holiday Schedule FlexibilityGovernment-announced holidays may shift the itinerary automatically — JourneyQuest will adjust as needed.

The Long HaulBoth international legs are ~26 hours door to door with 2 stops. Day 1 in Delhi is deliberately unscheduled in the morning to recover.

Asmi by Oberoi SpaThe Oberoi properties (Delhi, Rajvilas, Udaivilas, and Amarvilas in Agra) offer the Asmi wellness program — movement, nutrition, bodywork, breathwork, and mindfulness treatments. Ask Deepak to pre-book if interested.
